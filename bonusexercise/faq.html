NO QUESTION IS TOO SMALL

Questions? You are in good company.

First-year students ask a lot of the same things. Start with these answers, then use official Miami resources for details that change.

forum
GOOD TO KNOW

Frequently asked questions.

  • add_circle_outline How do I know which orientation steps apply to me?

    Open your personalized New Student Portal and check Miami’s official first-year orientation page. Your requirements may depend on your entry term or program.

  • add_circle_outline Where can I find my first-semester schedule?

    University online services such as myMiami and Banner Self Service provide student academic information. Check with an advisor if you have questions.

  • add_circle_outline How do I choose classes?

    Use academic advising and the new student academic guide. Advisors can help you understand requirements, placement results and credit.

  • add_circle_outline What should I bring to my residence hall?

    Start with everyday essentials, then verify your hall’s furniture, appliance restrictions, and move-in instructions before buying.

  • add_circle_outline Where can I find current dining hours?

    Use Miami’s official Dining Services website. Hours, locations and menus can change.

  • add_circle_outline How can I meet people during my first few weeks?

    Attend orientation and Welcome Weekend activities, introduce yourself to neighbors and classmates, and visit an organization meeting.

  • add_circle_outline What if I need help with a difficult class?

    Contact your instructor, visit office hours, talk with your advisor, and explore tutoring or coaching through the Rinella Learning Center.

  • add_circle_outline Where can I find support for my health or well-being?

    Use official Student Health Services or Student Counseling Service resources for eligibility, appointments, and current options.

  • help_outline How can I get involved on campus as a freshman?
    Miami University has many student organizations, clubs, and campus events. Freshmen can explore different organizations, attend campus events, and join activities that match their interests.

Need the official answer?

The university’s new student guidebook and orientation team are better sources for personal, time-sensitive, or policy questions.
